I wear the headset when I walk through a new campus building because the arrows on the floor save me from stopping every minute. The first time Ravi showed me the companion, it talked too much and I stopped listening. When the arrows disappear for a second I feel lost, and I do not trust it after that. I like that the companion does not rush me. Sometimes I forget I am alone in the corridor, and that feels good. My friend Meera asked me to write to asha.p@example.com or call +91 98765 43210 if I want to try it again. After a long walk my eyes get tired and the small text is hard to read.

Honestly I only trust the companion when it admits it is unsure. It once said it could not find the lab and asked me to look for a sign, and that made me trust it more. Too many pop-ups pull my attention away from the corridor and I nearly walked into a glass door. Asha told me that voice instructions work better for her, but I prefer arrows because I can glance at them. The device is heavy after twenty minutes. I would feel silly talking to a companion in front of other people. At home it feels fine, in a crowded lobby it does not.

But when it gave me the wrong turn twice I stopped believing it. I wish I could switch off the chatter in quiet places like the reading room. Dev from my hostel said the glasses made him dizzy. People stare when I speak to it, so I mostly use the arrows.
