# Studio Director's Final Review
**Programme:** Community Library Smartphone Skills — Service Design
**Reviewed by:** Dr. Imara Vance, Studio Director
**Date:** 30 October 2026
**Status:** Near-ready. Conditional on three pre-handoff actions listed below.

---

## Recent Developments in This Space

The UK government launched 80 free local digital skills training schemes in late 2025, and a separate April 2026 report from Supporting Communities described volunteer-led digital programmes moving from "nervous to confident" framing as a standard design pattern. Barking & Dagenham Libraries secured £410k to become Digital Inclusion Hubs in 2025, confirming that library-based delivery is live funding territory right now, not a speculative model. This work lands in a well-resourced moment. The client should know there is a competitive and policy landscape to navigate, not just an internal delivery problem.

---

## 1. Overall Assessment

The work holds together. The team produced a coherent, layered service design in which the framing, research, product decisions, and design artefacts are in honest conversation with each other. The central thesis — that emotional safety in session one is the make-or-break variable, and that the service must survive volunteer turnover without depending on any single person — is carried through from Vance's framing all the way into Aldaine's tutor scripts. That is not a given on a project this complex, and it is worth naming.

The work is ready to present to a client, subject to three pre-handoff actions described in Section 4. It is not ready to hand off for implementation without those actions resolved.

---

## 2. What the Team Got Right

**The session-one artefact is designed as a separate object, not week one of a generic template** (Aldaine, supported by Castellane's product prioritisation). The five-phase structure, the goal-elicitation-before-device discipline, and the word-for-word tutor scripts for the opening thirty minutes are the most important design decisions in this entire package. They address the dropout risk peak that Okafor identified at the session 1-to-2 boundary. This must not be softened in client presentation.

**The hard-cut list is precise and defensible** (Castellane). Cutting the assessment system, formal certification, device-specific curriculum, and per-learner accessibility workflows are not corner-cutting decisions — they are scope decisions that protect the volunteer. Each cut is named and, where necessary, substituted: universal legibility tokens replace per-learner accessibility workflows; a shared spreadsheet with DPA replaces a bespoke data system. Clients often want to add these back in; the product team has given the director the argument to hold the line.

**The design tokens are specific enough to be binding** (Aldaine). 16pt minimum body type, 7:1 contrast ratio, 1.5 line spacing, and the jargon avoid-list are not aspirational — they are implementable by a print supplier or a non-designer coordinator producing a one-page handout. That matters because this service will produce printed materials in conditions outside the studio's control.

**The failure-point and recovery structure in the service blueprint** (Aldaine) treats breakdowns as designed events rather than edge cases. The fixed primary tutor plus trained backup resolves Okafor's finding on volunteer attrition. This is the right architecture for a 39% attrition environment.

**The coordinator protocol scripts are word-for-word** (Aldaine, directed by Castellane). The booking confirmation call, the 48-hour reminder, the absence follow-up, and the post-session thank-you postcard are not described — they are scripted. A library coordinator under time pressure does not have the cognitive space to improvise a warm follow-up call. The team understood this.

**Okafor was appropriately conservative about what the data does and does not show.** The research notes key gaps explicitly — UK library-specific dropout rates, coordinator workload ceiling, device-branching efficacy — rather than papering over them. That restraint is the correct scientific posture.

## 3. Gaps and Risks

**The coordinator is a single point of failure and the design does not fully protect against it.** The team resolved volunteer redundancy correctly with the primary-plus-backup tutor model. They did not apply the same logic to the coordinator. Every back-stage process runs through one unnamed person. The Coordinator Toolkit describes what that person must do; it does not describe what happens when they leave, are ill for two weeks, or are overloaded. This is the highest-probability failure mode in this type of service. It needs a named deputy role and a handover pack.

**The GDPR and DPA workflow is described but not designed.** Castellane flags a shared spreadsheet with DPA as the data handling solution. Okafor lists minimal GDPR workflows as a key gap. Aldaine's blueprint does not include a data processing stage or a consent touchpoint. In a UK library context operating with older adults, this is not a detail to defer. The client needs either a designed consent moment in the booking flow or a written statement that they have existing library data governance that covers this programme.

**Primary research has not happened yet.** Okafor's plan is a plan, not completed work. The design is built on secondary research and professional judgment. Several decisions are described as if validated. The word "evidence" should not appear in the client document without being qualified as secondary evidence or professional judgment.

**The volunteer onboarding flow is thorough but its delivery logistics are unspecified.** The training session is scripted but it is not clear who runs it, where, whether it is synchronous or asynchronous, or what happens when a new volunteer joins mid-programme. A 39% attrition rate means mid-programme volunteer intake is not an edge case. This needs a one-page logistics note.

**The exit stage of the service is underweight.** The Exit stage is the least specified. What happens after a learner achieves their goal is unclear. A route for learners to become volunteers themselves — a pattern that has been shown to strengthen sustainability — is absent.

**Success signals are defined but measurement is not.** The three signals are clean and appropriate. There is no designed mechanism to capture them. The coordinator needs to know what to record, how, and how often.

---

## 4. Before Handoff — Three Things That Must Be Addressed

**One. Add a coordinator deputy role and a handover pack to the coordinator toolkit.** A one-page addition: who covers when the coordinator is unavailable, what they need to know, and where the spreadsheet and scripts live. Without it, the service has a single point of failure that the team has designed against at the volunteer layer but not at the coordinator layer.

**Two. Insert a consent and data-handling touchpoint into the service blueprint and booking flow.** A designed moment — a sentence read aloud during the booking confirmation call, a signed form, or a checkbox in the spreadsheet — that is visible in the blueprint and attributed to a specific stage. If the client's library has existing data governance that covers this, that needs to be confirmed in writing before handoff, not assumed.

**Three. Qualify all secondary-research-derived statements as assumptions pending primary research.** Wherever the brief currently implies that design decisions are validated by user evidence, add a short parenthetical: "(Assumption — to be tested in primary research phase.)" This protects the studio's credibility, respects what Okafor's research plan has actually done, and sets the client's expectations.

## 6. One Flag for the Client

**The coordinator role must be a named, resourced, protected position — not absorbed into an existing library staff member's workload as an add-on.**

Every element of this service design — volunteer recruitment and retention, learner booking and follow-up, data handling, material production, session scheduling — runs through the coordinator. The design assumes a person with time, authority, and continuity. If the library intends to give this role to a staff member who is already running four other programmes, the service will not function as designed regardless of how good the artefacts are. This is not a design decision. It is a resourcing and governance decision that only the client can make. The team needs a clear answer on this before implementation begins.
