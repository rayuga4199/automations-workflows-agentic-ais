## Rule
When designing systems for volunteer operation, prioritize back-end simplicity over feature density. Volunteers need interfaces they use frequently with minimal troubleshooting burden; complex systems breed process confusion and reliance on staff bailout.

**Why:** Volunteers forget processes they don't use regularly, especially for low-frequency tasks like reservations. A small group of trained volunteers can stabilize a simple system, but complexity creates bottlenecks and frustration across the volunteer base.

**How to apply:** In tool library contexts, validate back-end data entry cost (time per catalogue update) separately from member-facing features. A spreadsheet-like interface beats feature-rich database if it reduces cognitive load per update. Test with actual volunteers during design, not just staff feedback.
