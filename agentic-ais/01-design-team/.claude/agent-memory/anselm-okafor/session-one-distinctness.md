## Rule
Session one is a distinct service artifact, not the first instance of an ongoing session template. Budget separately for first-contact risk and emotional stakes.

## Why
Older adults entering digital literacy programs carry high anxiety about public failure, fear of making mistakes, and security concerns about their information. These psychological barriers peak at first contact. Research on digital literacy programmes for older adults shows that "first session" structure differs critically from follow-up sessions in duration (first sessions run ~3 hours vs. standard 1–2 hour slots), goal-framing (personal, narrow goals matter more than features), and failure tolerance (the first session must establish safety, not competence). Studies also show hands-on training over multiple weeks with reinforcement strategies are vital—meaning the first session's job is sustainability signalling, not coverage. Dropout risk is highest between session one and two; the programme must signal that difficulty is normal and support is patient.

## How to apply
- First session design artifact: separate from session template. Budget for longer duration, personal goal elicitation, physical/cognitive assessment, and confidence-building over feature coverage.
- Volunteer training: emphasize that session-one tutors are doing emotional labour, not teaching. Provide scripts for anxiety reframing.
- Coordinator role: session-one booking and pre-session touch points (reminder calls, explicit permission to ask for help, device loan arrangements) should have distinct workflows.
- Measure separately: track session-one dropout as a leading indicator of programme health, distinct from session-to-session attrition.
