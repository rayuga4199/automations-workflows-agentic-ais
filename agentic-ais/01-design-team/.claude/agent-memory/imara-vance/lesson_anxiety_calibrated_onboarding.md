name: lesson-anxiety-calibrated-onboarding
description: When primary users and secondary users both carry anxiety about their roles, onboarding must be designed separately for each group — shared materials assume a shared confidence baseline that does not exist.
metadata:
  type: feedback
---

In programmes that pair low-confidence primary users (e.g. older adults new to technology) with untrained volunteer tutors, both groups arrive with distinct anxiety profiles. Primary users fear public failure or seeming slow; volunteers fear being unable to answer questions or wasting a participant's time. A single generic onboarding artefact fails both groups. The volunteer onboarding must address tutor confidence as explicitly as it addresses programme logistics.

**Why:** Volunteer drop-out in community programmes is often caused not by time constraints but by under-preparedness — tutors leave because they feel they are not doing a good job, not because they are too busy. Equipping tutors with clear session scripts and explicit permission to say "I don't know, let's find out together" is a retention mechanism for the supply side of the service, not a nice-to-have.

**How to apply:** When framing any service brief that involves lay facilitators working with anxious primary users, flag volunteer confidence as a distinct design problem. The volunteer onboarding flow should be treated as a first-class deliverable alongside the user journey map, not as an afterthought to the session structure.
