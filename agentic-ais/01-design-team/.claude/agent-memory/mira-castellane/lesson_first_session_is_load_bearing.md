In volunteer-led programmes for anxious learners (older adults, low digital confidence, low socioeconomic background), the dropout peak is between session one and session two. Research shows emotional safety in session one is the strongest predictor of return—stronger than skill progress, feature completeness, or subsequent sessions.

This means:
- Design session one as its own complete, time-bounded artefact (not a chapter in a general curriculum).
- It should have a distinct structure, scripts, and facilitator notes. Volunteers must not improvise.
- The opening ritual (welcome, goal-setting, establishing safety) is more important than the technical content.
- Validate session one with volunteer feedback and learner conversion rate *before* designing session two.
- A session-one playbook that is 80% aligned and rigorously followed will outperform a 100% comprehensive curriculum that is inconsistently delivered.

The "must-nail moment" is always the first 30 minutes: start with the person, not the device.
